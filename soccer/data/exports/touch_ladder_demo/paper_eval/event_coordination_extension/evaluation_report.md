# Event-chain consistency and fixed-ball event-IK ablation — evaluation report

Sequence: `touch_ladder_demo` (one monocular clip, 144 frames, 24 fps, two G1 robots, 6 receive + 7 kick annotated
events, 6 kick→receive transfer pairs). Everything below is a **kinematic event-consistency** evaluation on a
**prescribed, event/scene-conditioned ball path**. No physics, no contact forces, no claim that the ball motion was
generated by foot–ball contact, no real-robot execution. One sequence is one sample; the 13 events / 6 pairs are
dependent sub-samples, so no standard deviations or significance claims are made.

Frozen inputs were not modified (hashes in `manifest.json` and `pipeline_dependency_audit.md`); the accepted Full demo,
its robots and its ball are read-only references throughout.

Companion files: `pipeline_dependency_audit.md` (Phase 1), `metric_definitions.md`, `event_metrics.csv`,
`transfer_chain_metrics.csv`, `event_chain_summary.csv`, `fixed_ball_ablation.csv`, `threshold_sensitivity.csv`,
`event_chain_metrics.json`, `interaction_timeline.{png,pdf}`, `qualitative_comparison.{png,pdf}`, `REPRODUCE.md`.

## 5. Answers to the six questions

**1. Does the complete system preserve the intended cross-agent receive/kick sequence?**
Yes, in the kinematic sense measured here. On the frozen Full output all 13 events have the annotated robot *and* foot
as the nearest of the four feet (receive 6/6, kick 7/7), the predicted closest-approach frame is within 0–3 frames of
the annotation (mean 0.17 receive / 0.57 kick frames), every predicted kick precedes its predicted receive, and all 6
kick→receive pairs are role-consistent (6/6, foot-role-only). Geometry-gated: 6/6 at τ = 0.05 m with the toe-centre
gate, 3/6 with the stricter mesh gate at 0.05 m (6/6 at 0.08 m, 1/6 at 0.03 m), because the foot mesh enters the ball
sphere by 1.5–5.5 cm at every event. Caveat: the ball was *generated* from these robots' feet at these frames, so
foot-role and timing consistency of the Full output is largely guaranteed by construction (see Q3); the informative part
is that the prescribed path does not re-assign any event to the wrong robot or foot and the frame-level ordering holds.

**2. Does event-conditioned robot refinement help when the object path is fixed?**
Not for actor/limb interaction consistency. With the ball, reference, initialisation and budget held fixed, OFF and ON
are identical on every assignment and timing metric (receive 1.00/1.00, kick 1.00/1.00, 4-way 1.00, timing 0.17/0.57
frames, role-only chain 6/6, centre-gated chain 6/6 at τ = 0.05 and 0.08). The differences are elsewhere: ON tracks the
reference kick-foot velocity much better (RMSE 0.20 vs 0.66 m/s, peak toe speed 3.95 vs 3.52 m/s against 4.07 m/s in
the reference) and reduces support-foot displacement slightly (0.446 vs 0.466 m), while OFF has the better foot–ball
surface relation (|dev| 0.015 vs 0.028 m; toe centre inside the ball at 6 vs 12 of 13 events; mesh-gated chain 5/6 vs
1/6 at τ = 0.05) and roughly half the jerk. So the event-conditioned IK terms behave as a *motion-fidelity* refinement
(faster, source-like kicks) rather than a *coordination* refinement.

**3. Which gains come from robot coordination rather than changes to the ball trajectory?**
Decomposing along the pipeline:
* basic_ik → event_semantics (own baseline ball, no stationing): kick association stays 4/7, role chain 3/6; surface
  deviation improves only 0.102 → 0.094 m. The IK event terms alone do not create coordination.
* event_semantics → full_without_position (same robots, ball re-snapped to them): kick association 4/7 → 7/7, role
  chain 3/6 → 6/6, |dev| 0.094 → 0.017 m. These gains come **entirely from regenerating the ball from the robot feet**
  (object-side, event-conditioned), not from robot motion.
* full_without_position → Full: lane error 0.201 → 0.015 m and cone clearance −0.129 → +0.015 m (ball-path/stationing
  properties); chain metrics unchanged at 6/6.
* fixed-ball OFF → ON (this report): no change in any assignment/timing/chain metric.
Therefore none of the measured chain-consistency gains can be attributed to event-conditioned *robot* refinement. What
is attributable to the robot side is kick-velocity fidelity (0.66 → 0.20 m/s RMSE) and a small reduction in
support-foot drift (0.47 → 0.45 m), at the cost of jerk and ball penetration. Where the event structure *does* act on the
robots is upstream, in the shared reference (event-timed stationing puts the annotated foot on the lane at the annotated
frame); that component could not be ablated without also moving the robots away from the frozen ball (§4.1 shows what
happens when the stationing differs: assignment drops to 0.83/1.00 and all geometry gates fail for both variants).

**4. What happens to jerk and geometric validity?**
Event IK roughly doubles jerk under identical inputs: root 436 → 797 m/s³, joint 585 → 1292 rad/s³ (the accepted Full
is at 784 / 1242). The sources are the smoothness relaxation inside kick windows and the kick-velocity term. Floor
validity is unaffected (keypoint proxy max 0.014 m in both; mesh floor max 0.0227 m in both, 0.0270 m on the accepted
Full). Ball-side validity gets worse with event IK: mean signed surface deviation −0.021 m (toe centre inside the sphere
at 12/13 events) vs +0.000 m for OFF (6/13); the foot mesh penetrates the ball by ≥ 0.03 m at 11 of 13 events for ON vs
5 for OFF, and the mesh gate at τ = 0.05 m passes 6/13 events for ON vs 12/13 for OFF. A smaller toe-centre distance (ON 0.089 m vs OFF 0.110 m) therefore does not indicate a better contact; it indicates
deeper interpenetration with the prescribed sphere.

**5. What evidence supports interaction-state representation as a useful coordination specification?**
Positive evidence is at the specification level, not at the IK-loss level: (i) the annotated receive/kick chain
(actor, foot, frame) is reproduced 13/13 and 6/6 by the complete system and remains 13/13 and 6/6 under both IK
settings when the specification (reference + ball) is held fixed, i.e. the chain is a stable property of the
specification; (ii) removing event conditioning from the *object* side (event_semantics vs full_without_position) breaks
kick association to 4/7 and role chain to 3/6 while the robot motion is identical, showing that the event structure is
what binds object and limb roles; (iii) with a mismatched stationing specification (§4.1) consistency drops for both IK
variants, again independent of the IK event terms. This supports the claim that the interaction-state representation is a
useful *coordination specification*; it does not support the claim that event-conditioned IK refinement improves
coordination.

**6. Which claims remain unsupported or inconclusive?**
* Any physical claim (contact, impulse, ball dynamics, sliding friction, balance) — not tested; the ball path is
  prescribed and the foot mesh is inside the sphere at most events.
* "Event-conditioned robot refinement improves cross-agent coordination" — **not supported** by the fixed-ball test
  (no change in any chain metric); supported only for kick-velocity fidelity.
* A clean event-only ablation — not achieved: the shared reference carries event-timed stationing and event-frame foot
  grounding, and the frozen ball was generated from robots tracking that reference. The reported comparison isolates the
  IK-side event terms only.
* Generalisation — single sequence, single annotation set, 9 distinct event frames; no population statistics.
* Exact reproducibility of the accepted Full from the current scripts — not achieved (audit §5; keypoint-level
  agreement ≈ 5 mm mean, joint-level mean |Δq| 0.013–0.018 rad, concentrated in the post-alignment ramp windows). The
  frozen Full remains the reference artifact and was not modified.
* Mesh-gated results at τ = 0.03 m are at the resolution limit of the vertex-sampled proxy and should not be
  over-interpreted.
